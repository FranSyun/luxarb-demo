What to check when buying a used Classic Flap

The bag on this page is a medium Classic Flap, also called a double flap: quilted leather, an interlocking CC turn-lock, and a chain that can be worn doubled or long. It is not a 2.55 reissue, which uses a different lock and often has leather woven through the chain. It is not a Boy bag, and it is not a Wallet on Chain.

  • Corners and the edge piping. Caviar’s grain hides light scuffs. Deep corner rubs, lifted edges, and a collapsed shape should be in the photos, not only in the grade.
  • The CC turn-lock. It should sit straight and close. A replated or loose clasp is a different condition story from light hardware scratches.
  • Chain links for stretch, kinks, or a color that does not match the rest of the hardware.
  • Interior stamp or chip, pockets, and smell. Many bags from 2021 on use a microchip instead of the older serial sticker. The Fashionphile listing here is a 2021 bag. Read whether that page mentions a chip, a card, or both. A missing sticker is not, by itself, a defect.
  • What is in the package. Fashionphile lists an authenticity card. Rebag lists no accessories. A card does not authenticate the bag by itself. LuxArb does not authenticate items.

No. Shops usually sort Classic Flaps into small (about 9 inches wide), medium (about 10 inches), and jumbo (about 12 inches). Confirm the centimeters on the listing. A mini rectangular flap is a different, smaller bag. This page is the medium size only.

Corners, edge piping, the CC turn-lock, chain links, interior, and any odor note. Caviar hides light rubs better than lambskin. Gold-tone hardware is a different specification from silver-tone. An authenticity card is not proof by itself. LuxArb does not authenticate bags.
