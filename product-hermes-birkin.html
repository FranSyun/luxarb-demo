What to check on a used Birkin 30

A Birkin 30 is the mid size: about 30 centimeters across the base, with two handles and a flap that covers the opening. It is not a Birkin 25 or 35, and it is not a Kelly. The two asks on this page share Togo leather, the color Noir (Hermès black), and palladium hardware, which is the silver-tone metal. Gold hardware would be a different specification.

  • Handles. Darkening can be use. Cracks, dryness, and a collapsed handle are condition. Read the photos, not only the grade.
  • Corners, base, and feet. Dragging wear shows up here first.
  • Shape. Togo holds a firmer line than Clemence, which slouches. Both listings here are Togo. A Clemence bag at a lower ask is not the same leather.
  • Hardware engraving and the interior stamp. The blind stamp is the year mark. Rebag prints stamp A and describes it as 2017. Use the stamp on the bag you are reading. Do not borrow a year from a different listing.
  • Smell, color transfer, and any replaced piece. Hermès shops are specific. A one-word grade does not replace those notes.

LuxArb does not authenticate Hermès. The shop’s process and return rules are what apply.

Questions

Is a Birkin 30 the same purchase as a Birkin 25?

No. The number is the size in centimeters. A 25, a 30, and a 35 are different bags. This page is a Birkin 30 in Togo, color Noir, with palladium hardware. A Kelly is a different silhouette. See the Kelly checks page before you mix those asks.

Why are these two Noir Togo Birkins not a matched pair?

Size, leather, color, and hardware line up. Year and accessories do not. What does a missing lock and keys change?

You are buying a different set. A full set usually means lock, keys, clochette, and dust bag, sometimes with the box and raincoat. Price the bag whose photos show those pieces. Do not add them in your head and then compare the asks.

Does LuxArb authenticate Hermès bags?

No. Use the shop’s authentication, the interior stamp photos, and that shop’s return policy.
