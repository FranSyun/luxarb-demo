Compare pre-owned luxury prices

Five snapshots you can open and verify: a Chanel Classic Flap, a Hermès Birkin 30, a Louis Vuitton Neverfull MM, a used Rolex Datejust 36, and an Omega Speedmaster.

A list-price gap is not savings and not profit. Condition, authenticity, shipping, and platform fees change the result. See the Disclaimer.

How to compare pre-owned luxury prices

Use this order: model, then condition, then fees. The cards below are captured asking prices from public listings, checked on 2026-10-29 (Asia/Shanghai).

Match the model before the price

Compare brand, model, size, material, color, and hardware or reference number. A Birkin 30 is not a Birkin 25. On the Rolex card, both watches are ref. 126200, but one dial is silver and the other is white Roman. That is not a matched pair. Sources: the Fashionphile and Chrono24 listings linked below.

Condition grades are not interchangeable

Fashionphile Excellent, Rebag Great, and Chrono24 “like new and unworn” are each platform’s own words. Read the photos and the written flaws. The Chanel card pairs a Fashionphile Excellent from 2021, with an authenticity card, with a Rebag Great that lists no accessories. Those grades are not a shared scale.

Fees, shipping, and duties

This site does not subtract costs. The numbers are list prices only. Add shipping, tax, duties, and a return-shipping reserve yourself. A gap of a few hundred dollars, like the Neverfull card, can vanish after those costs. This is not a profit estimate.

Why list prices differ

Supply, promotions, who pays the seller fee, and how strict the grade is all move the ask. Two listings can also be different years. The Birkin card is the same size, leather, color, and hardware, but a 2022 bag without lock and keys versus a 2017 stamp A with lock, keys, clochette, and box.

Questions about comparing pre-owned luxury prices

Why do list prices for the same luxury model differ across platforms?

The listings are often not the same physical item. Color, leather, year, hardware, reference, dial, and condition grade can all differ. Each platform also prices for its own buyers, fees, and promotions. A gap between two asks is a research signal, not a locked-in profit. On this page the Birkin 30 examples share size, Togo leather, Noir, and palladium hardware, but not the year or the accessories. Sources: Fashionphile, Rebag, and Chrono24 listings recorded by LuxArb on 2026-10-29.

Besides the list price, what costs matter when comparing secondhand luxury?

Shipping, sales tax, import duties, payment fees, return shipping, and any seller commission if you later resell. LuxArb shows asking prices only. A few hundred dollars of list-price difference — see the Neverfull card — can disappear once those costs are included.

When is a “same bag, same condition” comparison invalid?

When size, leather, color, hardware, reference number, dial, year, or included accessories differ, or when the platforms use different grade names. The Rolex card is the same reference (126200) with different dials: silver index on Fashionphile, white Roman on Chrono24. Those prices are not a matched pair.

What should a first-time buyer check on a used Chanel or Hermès?

The platform’s condition notes and photos (corners, edges, hardware, interior), the date or stamp the listing actually prints, and what is included (box, dust bag, lock, keys, card). Then read that platform’s authentication and return policy. LuxArb does not authenticate items.

Is buying on one luxury resale site and selling on another a guaranteed profit?

No. List prices move, items sell, condition disputes happen, and fees and duties are real costs. Nothing on LuxArb is financial advice.

How do duties, shipping, and currency affect a cross-border purchase?

Enough to erase a small gap. A watch shipping from another country can add insured shipping, VAT, or duty that the list price does not include. Confirm the total for your destination before you pay. The Omega listing checked here is a New York seller; another listing might not be.
